Well, here's the backstory.

You're a depressed 30 something year-old living life on your couch, eating pizza, drinking beer every day. You despise your job, but it pays the bills, so you can't complain.

Well, one day you saw a TV ad about a guy who started his own business, preaching how you can do it too.

So you take out your laptop, set your company colors and logo, and get to work. First, it's a friend's grandma, then a neighbour. And before you know it,
you have a fully fledged business on your hands. All it takes is a bit of hard work and determination.

When can we play it
---
Well, most of the game is actually finished. Now, after our last game, we admit we are worried about burnout a lot more than before. Last time it hit us like a train, and it began to do so with this one as well.

We both have regular day jobs, so coming home after 8 hours of work and then working some more is a challenge you need to learn how to handle.

Another thing is planning. Our last game was a mess when it came to planning. We were never on schedule with anything because nothing was tracked correctly. 

With this one, we wanted to make planning the main point of the process, and it panned out really well. Even though making the decision to stop development for a couple of months was difficult, we agreed it was the best decision at the time focusing on delivering quality over quantity.

Even with that hardship, we were actually quite quick with this project.
(*Development part incoming... You've been warned*)

We actually cleaned up a lot of our projects and are determined to keep that up as much as possible.

We introduced submodules for each global mechanic, so all of the systems that can be shared among other projects are now actual packages that can be updated and maintained individually, separately.

This helps us speed up the development process a lot more and keep the rest of the project bug-free, since we know so many modules are individually battle-tested, so we don't have to worry about them as much.
